# Podcast from meeting content

Date: 2026-11-21. Status: **planned**. The seven concepts, audio formats, workflows, and tests below are proposals. The speech routes are candidates from the supplied catalog snapshot, with browser access and quality unverified. See [product requirements](PRODUCT.md), [model exploration](MODEL-EXPLORATION-2026-11-21.md), and [quality evaluation](QUALITY-EVALUATION.md).

## Shared design and safeguards

- **Source and script.** Start from a human-reviewed memo version and its exact transcript revisions. The LLM gets an explicit selection of approved memo fields and the minimum cited excerpts needed for the task. Each factual script line carries a memo item ID and exact source revision references in a sidecar. Keep proposals, decisions, conditions, dissent, and superseded claims distinct. Mark an exact quote in writing and announce it as a quotation in audio. Paraphrases get no quotation marks. Keep missing owners, dates, amounts, speaker identity, and answers unknown. Mark planned coverage by selected items and list gaps. If a source revision changes, keep the earlier artifact and flag it as stale. Human review of the exact script precedes speech generation and export.
- **Voices.** Use stock synthetic narrators and label them as synthetic in the intro, player, and export. Pick conversational roles such as Host and Guide, with no real-world identity claims. Exclude participant audio, voiceprints, custom voice enrolment, and prompts requesting resemblance to a participant. Check stock voices with a human reviewer for accidental resemblance and use a different approved voice when flagged. Keep transcript attribution separate from narration and retain uncertainty, including overlap.
- **Consent.** Default to a browser-first PWA with runtime BYOK for cloud stages. Before the script request, show the exact selection, destination, selected model, known upstream and retention or their unknown status, and an estimate or unknown charge. Before speech synthesis, show the exact approved text, destination, model, voice choices, and possible charge. Edits or route changes reset the affected confirmation. A free route still sends content and requires consent. Local previews, cancellations, and offline views send nothing. Sharing is an explicit local download after a separate audience preview. Keep internal citations in an opt-in sidecar where they would expose private evidence.
- **Costs and recovery.** Budget for script input/output tokens, TTS input/output under the route's own units, and extra synthesis after revisions. Show reported usage and charge when available, and label calculated estimates with the applicable rate and date. Save request intent before submitting and retain returned paid output for local inspection even if it fails an acceptance check. Check size and format eligibility where possible before submission, bound each request, and reuse completed audio. Unknown outcomes need billing review and explicit consent before any new paid attempt, with zero SDK or automatic submission retries and no silent model fallback. A podcast draft must never refresh the existing Pi plan or reset its 100 cumulative attempts across continuation, reload, or backup.
- **Offline and export.** Plan for on-device drafts and approved audio in app-scoped local storage, with bounded storage and visible save and recovery states. Playback and downloads use locally saved media while it remains available. Pause generation offline and preserve drafts through an app update. Proposed audio container is MP3 where delivery or browser conversion is validated. Record the actual format and gate promotion on target-browser playback and download. Optional sidecars carry script, citations, and chapter indexes. Use generated-audio offsets for chapters and source-audio offsets only where the original recording has valid timing. Keep provider keys out of every export and cache. Installed-phone, offline, and audio format behavior remain unverified.

## 1. Three-minute action-item brief

- **Value and sources.** Catch absent attendees up on what to do next. Feed accepted action items (task, owner, date, dependencies), related decisions and conditions, and open questions that block action.
- **Script and voices.** Ask the LLM to group tasks by confirmed priority or memo order and attach each spoken action field to its supporting citation. Say “owner unknown” or “due date unknown” where required. A single stock narrator could use `google/gemini-3.8-flash-tts` or `microsoft/mai-voice-2.1-flash`.
- **Consent and cost.** “Send the selected actions, linked decisions and cited excerpts to your script provider. After review, send the approved brief to your speech provider.” Token volume scales with the selected evidence and script, and synthesis with the length of the approved clip. Show any revised-clip charge before sending.
- **Offline and export.** Save the draft and finished brief for local playback. Export an audio file and optionally a cited Markdown action list.
- **Risks and acceptance test.** A speaker can be miscast as the owner, a tentative date can become a deadline, and the audience may be excluded from a sensitive action. Use a fixture with an unassigned task, a later corrected deadline, and an excluded action. Pass when the reviewed brief lasts within 3:00, preserves the unknown and corrected date with valid citations, and excludes the sensitive item. Report any approved actions omitted for the time limit.

## 2. Two-host decision recap

- **Value and sources.** Explain what was decided and why in a conversational recap. Use accepted decisions, discussion rationale, conditions, dissent, risks, and unresolved questions.
- **Script and voices.** Have the LLM write a Host and Guide exchange, citing each factual line. Attribute paraphrases to the reviewed memo or confirmed speaker attribution when supported, and announce any exact quotation. Compare `fish-audio/s2.1-pro` as a dialogue candidate with separate stock turns via `google/gemini-3.8-flash-tts`, subject to route verification.
- **Consent and cost.** “Send the selected decisions, rationale and cited excerpts to your script provider. After review, send the approved dialogue and stock voice choices to your speech provider.” Track script length, synthesis units, and the effect of turn splitting or regenerating one turn.
- **Offline and export.** Draft and replay finished conversations locally. Export a continuous audio file and a labelled transcript with optional evidence references.
- **Risks and acceptance test.** Banter may turn proposals into decisions or make the hosts sound like participants. Use a fixture with a rejected proposal, conditional approval, and an exact quote. Pass when both roles are clearly labelled synthetic, the approval retains its condition, the quotation is marked and exact, and the rejected proposal stays rejected. Human reviewers check for voice resemblance.

## 3. On-demand topic chapters

- **Value and sources.** Let listeners revisit one part of a long meeting without playing a full recap. Group accepted discussion items, decisions, actions, and risks by topic and retain links to the relevant portions of the recording.
- **Script and voices.** Ask the LLM for a short, independently citable introduction and recap per chosen topic. Include cross-topic corrections when cited. Compare `google/gemini-3.8-flash-lite-tts` and `elevenlabs/eleven-v3` for short stock narration.
- **Consent and cost.** “Send only the topics and evidence you selected to your script provider. Send each approved chapter script to your speech provider under this confirmed scope.” Chapter count and length affect tokens and audio generation. Generate only selected chapters and reuse completed clips on local retries.
- **Offline and export.** Play downloaded or locally saved chapters in order or independently. Export chapter audio files with a local JSON chapter index and optional cited text.
- **Risks and acceptance test.** Chapter boundaries can hide qualifications or suggest fabricated source timing. Use an untimed cited passage, a correction under a later topic, and an unselected topic. Pass when the corrected fact is cited, the untimed source opens as text, the excluded topic is absent, and each generated chapter can be selected after reload offline.

## 4. Weekly digest across selected meetings

- **Value and sources.** Give a project team a summary of changes across meetings. Use reviewed summaries, decisions, outstanding actions, and risks from an explicit set of meetings, each with its own revision and date.
- **Script and voices.** Have the LLM group updates by topic and anchor each claim to its meeting and exact revision. Report conflicting ownership or dates as unresolved unless reviewed evidence explicitly supersedes them. Compare `elevenlabs/eleven-v4` and `microsoft/mai-voice-2.1-flash` as stock narration candidates.
- **Consent and cost.** “Send the displayed fields and evidence from these selected meetings to your script provider. After review, send the approved digest to your speech provider.” Show the meeting list and omissions. Cost grows with selected meetings, cited evidence, and digest duration.
- **Offline and export.** Keep the selection and saved audio local. Export an audio file and a separate optional cross-meeting evidence index. Any sharing follows audience review.
- **Risks and acceptance test.** Combining meetings can expose private material or collapse people with the same name. Use two selected meetings with contradictory dates and same-name attendees, plus an excluded meeting. Pass when the output preserves the contradiction, makes no cross-meeting speaker identification, and omits the excluded meeting from the request and exports.

## 5. Read the approved memo with citation chapters

- **Value and sources.** Listen to an auditable version of the existing memo. Use the approved sections in order: summary, discussion, decisions, actions, open questions, and risks.
- **Script and voices.** Ask the LLM to add section transitions and turn citation references into navigation metadata. Preserve accepted wording, amounts, and conditions. Human review checks any pronunciation gloss and retains the original text. Compare `hexgrad/kokoro-82m` for a possible local route (browser runtime and license unverified) with a stock `google/gemini-3.8-flash-tts` route.
- **Consent and cost.** “Send the displayed memo sections and cited excerpts to your script provider. For cloud narration, send the approved read-aloud script to your speech provider.” A verified local voice would keep the script on device. Costs scale with memo length and synthesized output, or with device storage and computation for a local voice.
- **Offline and export.** Keep the reviewed script and saved audio available offline. Export audio and a chapter index pairing generated offsets with exact source references, with source timestamps only where supported.
- **Risks and acceptance test.** Spoken numbers or conditions can drift and citations can expose internal text. Use a reviewed memo with a precise monetary amount, a condition, and an untimed citation. Pass when the recording preserves the exact meaning, chapter seek works, and references resolve to the frozen memo version without inventing source timing.

## 6. Accessibility listen mode

- **Value and sources.** Give users a readable script, predictable playback, and a voice option for approved summaries, actions, and questions. Let users select sections and control playback pace.
- **Script and voices.** Ask the LLM for short speakable sentences with line citations, explicit unknowns, and no meaning changes. Prefer a device voice only after proving it keeps the script local and works offline on the tested platform. Check `microsoft/mai-voice-2.1-flash` and `elevenlabs/eleven-flash-v2.5` as cloud alternatives with stock voices.
- **Consent and cost.** “Use the selected text on this device when an offline voice is verified. Cloud script drafting or narration sends the displayed text to the selected provider after confirmation.” Keep a reviewed text-only option if no eligible voice is available. Compare LLM and cloud synthesis charges with any local storage, model download, and device costs.
- **Offline and export.** Support reading, accessible playback controls, and replay of stored audio. Offer an accessible HTML or plain-text script and an audio download where bytes are available and verified for export.
- **Risks and acceptance test.** Device voices may involve unverified external processing, and audio-only status can hide failure. Test offline with a saved script and clip, keyboard and screen reader, and large text in installed-phone mode. Pass when play, pause, seek, and citation navigation are available, focus stays predictable, and an unverified device voice makes no request and offers the text view.

## 7. Question-and-answer rehearsal

- **Value and sources.** Prepare someone to brief a stakeholder. Use accepted decisions, action items, open questions, and risks, with the relevant cited discussion for context.
- **Script and voices.** Ask the LLM to write likely questions as hypothetical and ground each answer in cited sources. Say “the meeting did not establish that” when the evidence is insufficient. Use two stock roles via `elevenlabs/eleven-v4-turbo` or test separately generated turns with `x-ai/grok-voice-tts-1.0`.
- **Consent and cost.** “Send the selected decisions, actions, risks and cited context to your script provider. After review, send the approved questions and answers to your speech provider.” Track the number and length of pairs, evidence tokens, speech output, and any repeated clips.
- **Offline and export.** Save replayable Q&A clips and the cited rehearsal script. Export clips with Markdown question cards and optional evidence references.
- **Risks and acceptance test.** A hypothetical question can sound like a real stakeholder request, and an invented answer can be treated as a commitment. Use an unanswered pricing question and an accepted action. Pass when the rehearsal identifies the prompt as hypothetical, keeps the price unknown, and cites the action accurately.

## Ranked recommendation and two-week spike (planned)

1. **Three-minute action-item brief.** Start here. One approved meeting and one stock narrator make it practical to verify critical fields, audience scope, consent, and local recovery.
2. **Read the approved memo with citation chapters.** Reuse the reviewed script and evidence anchors with little new interpretation.
3. **On-demand topic chapters.** Add selective listening and test bounded exports.
4. **Accessibility listen mode.** Evaluate device voice privacy and accessible playback before making availability claims.
5. **Two-host decision recap.** Test dialogue only after the single-voice citation path holds up.
6. **Question-and-answer rehearsal.** Require evidence for answers and test abstention before promotion.
7. **Weekly digest.** Defer until cross-meeting authorization, relevance, and revision handling can be evaluated.

**Week 1.** Define a small one-meeting action-brief fixture set with unknown owners, corrected dates, stale citations, excluded details, and failed local saves. Prototype script, evidence sidecar, and two consent screens using only local or mocked inputs. Check cancellation, revision changes, frozen source links, save/export of returned output, and zero automatic POST duplication after timeout, reload, or backup restore.

**Week 2.** Compare one-voice candidates only if separately authorized and eligible under the [model protocol](MODEL-EXPLORATION-2026-11-21.md#evaluation-protocol). Use the same reviewed script and collect blinded fidelity and listening scores, actual route limits, and offline playback, keyboard, safe-area, and export checks on target browsers and physical phones. Without live authorization, record `NOT_RUN` for route evidence and complete only fixture-based checks. The proposed exit is a measured go/no-go for a private action-brief pilot. Require exact source links, zero critical changes or unauthorized disclosure in the release fixtures, retention of completed artifacts, and explicit device or provider gaps before making any support claim.
